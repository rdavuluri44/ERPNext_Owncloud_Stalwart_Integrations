# Onboarding to ERP Mobile: identity check, biometrics and re-onboarding

**Status:** draft, applies when built (P82) · **Owner of this SOP:** HR · **Last reviewed:** 26 November 2026

## Purpose

A sign-in key in ERP Mobile cannot be shared or copied, but it accepts **every** fingerprint and strong face
enrolled on the phone, and the system cannot see who the person really is. Two checks therefore stay with people:
that the person is who they say they are, and that only their own fingerprints and face are on the phone. If they
are skipped, a family member, a colleague or an impostor can sign in as the employee.

## Who

- **Employee:** requests access and confirms their phone.
- **Project manager** (of the employee's site or department): checks identity in person.
- **Admin:** approves the access in the sign-in service.

## When

- A new employee needs the app.
- **Re-onboarding:** after the app erased the key (5 wrong fingerprints in a row), after the sign-in server removed
  a key that was not used for 7 days (for example after leave or illness), or for a new, lost or replacement phone.

## Steps

1. The employee installs ERP Mobile, opens **Request access** and enters their work mail address, mobile number and a
   face photo. The app refuses phones without a strong fingerprint sensor or strong face unlock; such a phone cannot
   be used.
2. The project manager meets the employee **in person** and compares them with an original identity document and
   the face photo in the request.
3. On the employee's phone, together, open the phone's own security settings and list the enrolled fingerprints and
   faces. **Remove every one that is not the employee's** (family members, a previous owner). If the employee will
   not remove them, stop: the request is refused.
4. The employee confirms in writing that only their own fingerprints and face are enrolled and that they will not
   add anyone else's (adding one later destroys the key and means onboarding again).
5. The project manager approves the identity in the request. The face photo is used only for this check and is not
   kept after the request is decided.
6. The admin approves the request in the sign-in service. The app then creates the key on the phone and signs the
   employee in.
7. **Re-onboarding:** the same steps 2–6. The admin also checks that the old key was removed from the employee's
   account (the new approval removes it), and, for a lost or stolen phone, that all the employee's sessions were
   ended.

## Checks

- Identity document seen in original, matching the person and the request.
- No fingerprint or face on the phone other than the employee's.
- Written confirmation signed.
- The employee can sign in to the app with their fingerprint after the admin's approval.

## Record

- The project manager's approval and the admin's approval are recorded by the system with their names and times.
- The written confirmation (step 4), **proposal (owner to confirm):** given in the app, not on paper. The employee
  reads the text below and confirms it with their own fingerprint; the project manager then ticks that they saw the
  phone's list. The sign-up service stores it with the access request, linked to the employee in the ERP: the text
  version, both names and the time. Kept while the person is employed and for 3 years after they leave (to answer a
  later dispute), then deleted.
- No copy of the identity document and no face photo is kept.

### Confirmation text (draft 1, 26 November 2026)

> I confirm that the only fingerprints and face enrolled on this phone are my own, that I removed any others before
> this onboarding, and that I will not let anyone else add theirs. I understand that adding another fingerprint or
> face makes the app delete my sign-in key, and that I then have to be onboarded again.

## When it goes wrong

- Identity doubtful, or the person refuses step 3: refuse the request and tell HR.
- The phone has no strong sensor: the employee needs another phone; tell HR.
- After 5 wrong fingerprints the key is erased: the employee starts again at step 1 (common with wet, dusty or cut
  fingers; dry the finger and the sensor first).
- Back after more than 7 days away: the key was removed by the sign-in server; the app says so. Onboard again from
  step 1; plan it for the first day back.
- Lost passkey, or a new or replacement phone (owner's rule, 26 November 2026): the project manager checks the
  person's phone in person and confirms the request in their own app; nothing is re-issued on a message or a call.
- Phone lost or stolen: the employee tells the admin at once; the admin removes the key and ends the sessions, then
  re-onboarding.
